# RFC 2 — Dimension specifications

*Status: golden (merged from both drafts, grounded 2026-07-27) · Part 2 of 6*
*Reference material; grows per dimension as it ships. Depends on RFC 0. Levers in RFC 1.*

---

## How to read this

One section per dimension: canonical entities, the granularity ladder, canonical unit
metrics, target notes, allocation notes, simulation levers, compliance notes where they
apply, and two grounding blocks the drafts lacked —

- **Can we generate it?** What `dataraum-testdata` emits with known truth **today**. Read
  this as a work item, not a verdict: the generator is ours, it is cheap to extend, and
  extending it is the only thing that makes a dimension gradeable. A "no" here is a
  sprint, not a blocker (see *The generator is a design surface* below).
- **Can we demo it?** What public data exists. **Every external-corpus claim below is
  unverified from this repo** — on disk we have `corpora/relbench/` (rel-avito, rel-event,
  rel-f1, rel-hm, rel-salt, rel-stack, rel-trial) and `corpora/rwd/`, nothing else. The
  claims are carried forward from the source draft as *inputs to a licence-and-content
  audit*, not as facts. See RFC 3's work items.

Canonical metrics are **templates, not requirements**. A metric appears only where the
recovered model supports it.

**The tier discipline that governs this whole document** (`entropy_eval_architecture.md`):
*Tier A* — synthetic, generated by us, full truth, the only place recall and value
correctness are assertable. *Tier B* — wild, real, **structural truth only** (declared
FKs, types, time columns), a scoreboard that can falsify "we're great" but can never
certify a metric is computed right, because nobody knows the true answer. A public
purchasing corpus makes Supply *demoable*; it does not make Supply *correct*.

## Demand — "Your customers"

**Canonical entities.** Customer, account, segment, channel, region, quote, order, order
line, contract.

**Ladder.** Customer → segment → channel → region, with order and order line as the leaf
grain.

| Metric | Definition | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Revenue per customer / segment | revenue ÷ active entity | trivial, but the ladder entry point |
| Cost-to-serve | allocated operating cost ÷ customer or channel | the cross-dimension metric; requires an allocation scheme |
| Customer concentration risk | share of revenue in top 5% of customers | tracked by every executive, automated by none |
| Order-to-cash duration | order placement → settlement | shared with Capital; same fact, two readings |
| Retention / cohort survival | active entities surviving period n | contract archetype only; dark elsewhere |

**Target notes.** Peer comparison is unusually strong here — segments are natural siblings.
Plan data is common at aggregate level and almost never at customer level, so this
dimension is a frequent *partial*.

**Allocation notes.** Cost-to-serve is the flagship allocation and the hardest one: freight
by weight vs. by revenue vs. by order count produces three defensible and different
customer rankings. **Ship revenue-per-customer and DB1 first; cost-to-serve after the
allocation object exists.**

**Simulation levers.** Volume, price, mix (channel shift), allocation key.

**Can we generate it?** **No.** There is no customer entity in the generator, and invoices
are vendor-side (`Invoice.vendor_id`, a bare string with no vendor table). The `Order` /
`Delivery` / `Address` models are empty skeletons for the role-playing-FK probe, carrying
dates and FK roles but no money and no counterparty. Extending the generator with a
customer → order → order-line → revenue chain is the single highest-leverage testdata item
in this document: it is what makes Demand, Offer and the AR half of Capital gradeable at
once.

**Can we demo it?** Strong on paper. AdventureWorks OLTP (MIT) is the commercially clean
option; **rel-salt is CC-BY-NC-SA and cannot carry a commercial demo** — internal
evaluation only, which is exactly the tier our own licence policy already assigns it.
Northwind and ClassicModels are structurally fine and too small to look serious.

## Offer — "What you sell"

**Canonical entities.** Product, product group, category, service, SKU, contract type,
bundle, price list.

**Ladder.** Category → product group → product → variant/SKU.

| Metric | Definition | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Contribution margin ladder (DB1/DB2/DB3) | revenue − variable cost, then step-fixed layers | the canonical template; the disclosed calculation is the point |
| Price realization | invoiced price ÷ list price | catches discount leakage; almost never visible in ERP reporting |
| Mix effect | the mix term of the price–volume–mix bridge | the reason Offer is its own dimension |
| Margin per unit of constrained resource | contribution ÷ the archetype's binding capacity unit | Theory-of-Constraints native; the most under-computed metric in industry |
| Portfolio tail | share of SKUs below contribution threshold | pairs with concentration risk on the Demand side |

**Target notes.** Prior-period comparison at product level is noisy where the assortment
churns; entity birth and death (RFC 1) is a correctness requirement here, not an edge case.

**Allocation notes.** DB2 and DB3 are allocation steps by definition. The ladder *is* the
disclosure surface. DB1 is allocation-free and therefore shippable first.

**Simulation levers.** Price, mix, volume, allocation key.

**Can we generate it?** **No** — no product, no quantity, no price, no unit of measure.
Ships with the same generator extension as Demand.

**Can we demo it?** AdventureWorks carries products with standard cost and list price plus
cost/price history, which supports a real DB1 and a defensible constructed DB2. Nothing
public gives a genuine step-fixed cost layer; DB3 is synthetic everywhere and must be
labelled.

## Supply — "Your suppliers"

**Canonical entities.** Supplier, vendor, carrier, subcontractor, purchase order, PO line,
goods receipt, supplier invoice, claim, quality notification.

**Ladder.** Supplier group → supplier → PO → PO line, with carrier and site as cross-cuts.

| Metric | Definition | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Effective cost per unit delivered | price + delay cost + claim cost, per unit | the flagship. No ERP computes it; every procurement head wants it |
| On-time in-full (OTIF) | receipts matched against PO promise date and quantity | requires the receipt↔PO-line join, the usual missing link |
| Claim-to-spend ratio | claim cost ÷ supplier invoice value | needs a QA or complaint source |
| Price variance | invoice price − PO price, per line | pure ERP, always computable where invoices are connected |
| Lead-time variability | spread of promise-to-receipt, not the mean | the mean is a vanity metric; the spread drives buffer stock |

**Target notes.** Peer comparison is the natural target (supplier X vs. supplier Y for the
same material) and needs no plan data. This makes Supply the dimension that looks best
earliest — on a demo. It is not the dimension that grades earliest.

**Allocation notes.** Delay cost and claim cost must be allocated to a supplier through an
explicit rule. Buffer-stock cost attributable to lead-time variability is the hard case and
ships as a named optional scheme, never a default.

**Simulation levers.** Price, timing (lead time), volume (dual-sourcing as a mix lever),
rate (defect rate).

**Can we generate it?** **No** — there is no vendor master, no PO, no goods receipt.
`Invoice.vendor_id` is a string. A minimal Supply generator is a second, separate
extension after Demand/Offer.

**Can we demo it? (unverified)** The source draft's revision claims three usable corpora:
BPI Challenge 2019 (CC BY 4.0, purchase-to-pay event log with vendor, goods receipt,
invoice, EUR value and explicit three-way-match flows), AdventureWorks Purchasing (MIT,
with a real `RejectedQty` quality signal), and SCMS/USAID shipment pricing (CC-BY, with
scheduled vs. actual delivery dates), optionally joined to openFDA enforcement for an
external claim signal. Olist is excluded on licence (CC BY-NC-SA), as is any NC corpus.
**All of this is Tier B**: it can carry a demo and falsify a detector, and it certifies
nothing about metric correctness.

## Capacity — "What you run on"

**Canonical entities.** Machine, line, cell, vehicle, site, warehouse, room/bed/seat,
licence, role-hour pool · downtime event, maintenance order, shift calendar.

**Ladder.** Site → line → machine, or site → fleet → vehicle, or practice → grade →
role-hour pool.

| Metric | Definition | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Cost per capacity hour | full cost ÷ available hours | the generalized unit; works for a machine, a truck, a room and a consultant grade |
| Utilization | used ÷ available, against a stated theoretical ceiling | the ceiling must be explicit and versioned; most disputes are about the denominator |
| Output per unit of capacity | throughput ÷ capacity unit | pairs with Offer's margin-per-constrained-resource |
| Mean time between failures | operating time ÷ downtime events | asset archetypes only |
| Maintenance cost index | maintenance order cost ÷ replacement value | catches run-to-failure economics |

**Target notes.** The theoretical ceiling is a *definitional* Soll — typed distinctly so it
is never confused with plan (RFC 1).

**Allocation notes.** Shared capacity across product groups is the allocation problem here,
and it is the input to Offer's DB ladder. **The two dimensions must reference one scheme,
not two** — which is an argument for the allocation object being global, not per-dimension.

**Simulation levers.** Capacity (add a shift, add a line), rate (uptime, cycle rate),
timing (maintenance interval).

**Can we generate it?** **No**, and it is the largest generator gap: assets, meters,
downtime events and maintenance orders with cost are a whole new event family.

**Can we demo it? (unverified)** The source draft's revision claims Cincinnati Fleet
Services (public domain, ~332k maintenance work orders with labour/parts/total cost and
downtime hours, plus asset-months with fuel and depreciation) as a corpus where every
metric in this spec is a literal column, with Hill of Towie (CC-BY-4.0 wind SCADA) as a
second archetype and US BTS aviation as an allocation showcase. If it holds, Capacity is
the best-covered *demo* dimension. It remains ungradeable until a generator exists.

## Throughput — "How work flows"

**Canonical entities.** Process step, work order, operation, shift, line, team, project,
task, WIP position. **Never individual.**

**Ladder.** Value stream → process → step, with shift/team/project as categorical
cross-cuts.

| Metric | Definition | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Cost per process step | allocated cost ÷ completed steps | the unit |
| Cycle-time efficiency | standard duration ÷ actual duration | needs a standard; prior period substitutes |
| Yield / first-pass rate | good output ÷ total attempts | scrap and rework are the cost translation |
| Rework cost per unit | rework hours × rate ÷ good units | turns a quality conversation into a money conversation |
| Utilization × realization (people archetype) | billable ÷ available, invoiced ÷ billable | computed at grade or team level, never person |
| Cost at completion vs. plan (project archetype) | earned value against booked cost | the project archetype's binding metric |

**Compliance notes.** Person-level bindings are quarantined by default (§87 BetrVG, GDPR).
The design that keeps this dimension useful for people-intensive businesses is aggregation
to **role, grade, team or project** — every metric an executive acts on, none a works
council objects to. State it as the design, not a degraded mode.

**This is a build item with a gate, not a schema convention.** The engine binds discovered
columns and has no person/PII typing; a partner's `worker_id` would be bound like any other
dimension today. Throughput ships only with: a typed person-grain marker, a binding-time
refusal, and an eval oracle proving the refusal fires on a corpus that contains a person
column.

**Target notes.** Standards are the natural target where they exist (routings, standard
times). Where they do not, peer comparison across lines or teams beats prior period.

**Simulation levers.** Rate (yield, cycle time), capacity (shift structure), timing
(sequencing, batch size).

**Can we generate it?** **No.**

**Can we demo it? (unverified)** The source draft reports the shape as available and the
money as not: AdventureWorks `WorkOrder` / `WorkOrderRouting` carry real schedule variance
and scrap reasons but `ActualCost == PlannedCost` on every routing row, so **cost variance
is the one figure that must be synthesized and disclosed**; the 4TU production log has true
shop-floor vocabulary but 225 cases, no money and a non-open licence; BPI 2018 (CC0) has a
committed-vs-actual-vs-penalty triple. Note that the 4TU log carries `Worker ID` — which
makes it a good public demonstration of the quarantine rule rather than a problem.

## Capital — "Where your cash sits"

**Canonical entities.** Inventory position, stock movement, receivable, payable, work in
progress, capex item, asset register entry, credit line.

**Ladder.** Total working capital → component (inventory / receivables / payables) →
entity (SKU, customer, supplier) → item.

| Metric | Definition | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Cash conversion cycle | DIO + DSO − DPO | the anchor metric; decomposable down the ladder |
| Days inventory by SKU / group | inventory ÷ COGS run rate | where dead stock becomes visible as money, not units |
| Days sales outstanding by customer | receivables ÷ revenue run rate | pairs with Demand's cost-to-serve for a true customer ranking |
| Days payable by supplier | payables ÷ spend run rate | pairs with Supply's effective cost |
| Capital per unit of output | capital employed ÷ output | the turnover term made operational |
| Inventory carrying cost | holding rate × average inventory value | the rate is an assumption; a named, versioned parameter |

**Target notes.** Prior period is strong; peer comparison across SKUs and customers is
strong; plan data almost never exists at this grain. Expect permanent *partial* on the plan
branch and design for it.

**Allocation notes.** Little allocation, much attribution: assigning a receivable to a
segment or an inventory position to a product group is a join, not a key choice.

**Simulation levers.** Timing (terms, reorder point, batch size), volume, rate (carrying
cost, service level).

**Can we generate it? Partly — and this dimension is already the live example of the
coverage map working.** `dso`, `dio`, `dpo` and `cash_conversion_cycle` ship as metric
templates in the finance vertical. On our own corpus: the payables side has invoices,
payments and bank transactions, so DPO has substrate; **DIO has no inventory table to
ground against**; and because invoices are vendor-side, **the AR half of DSO does not
exist**. Applying RFC 0's coverage gate to the shipped Capital templates today therefore
yields *partial* honestly, without inventing anything — which is exactly the demo. It is
also a defect list: metric templates that cannot ground should say so rather than compute
something.

**Can we demo it? (unverified)** AdventureWorks carries inventory positions and a costed
transaction history sufficient for days-inventory and capital-per-unit-of-output, but has
**no AP subledger and no general ledger**, so DPO and a true working-capital statement need
another source (Wide World Importers is the named candidate). Carrying-cost rates are an
assumption everywhere and ship as named, versioned parameters.

## Cross-dimension structure

The dimensions are entity dimensions; the interesting facts are their intersections:

- **Supply × Demand** — did supplier claims cause order delays, and for which customers?
- **Supply × Capital** — is the cheapest supplier cheapest after payment terms and buffer stock?
- **Capacity × Throughput** — does one shift pattern produce more downtime than another, at team level?
- **Offer × Capacity** — which product group earns most per hour of the binding constraint?
- **Demand × Capital** — which customers are profitable on margin and unprofitable on cash?

Each is a single query in the unit-economics grammar **once both dimensions are lit, they
share a conformed dimension, and one allocation scheme spans them.** That is not a
metaphor: the conformed-dimension surface (`og_conformed_dimension`, the bus matrix) is the
engine's existing cross-fact join substrate, and its first consumer is DAT-809. Two
concrete consequences:

1. Cross-dimension comparison is gated on **conformance**, not on both dimensions merely
   existing. A customer key that does not conform across the Demand fact and the Capital
   fact yields no cross-dimension answer, and the coverage map should say which key is
   missing.
2. This is the strongest argument for RFC 0's ruling that dimensions are facets inside one
   vertical. Across workspaces there is no bus matrix and no shared allocation scheme, so
   none of the five queries above is expressible.

## Data availability summary

Licence is a column because it is a binding constraint: the most complete sources in two
categories (Olist for Supply, rel-salt for Demand) are non-commercial. **Content and
licence claims for corpora not on disk are unverified here.**

| Dimension | Generatable with truth today | Best commercially-usable public source (unverified) | Remaining gap |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Demand | **no** | AdventureWorks OLTP (MIT) | no customer/revenue chain in the generator; rel-salt blocked by NC licence |
| Offer | **no** | AdventureWorks (cost + price history) | no product/price/quantity in the generator; DB3 synthetic everywhere |
| Supply | **no** | BPI 2019 (CC BY 4.0) + AdventureWorks purchasing + SCMS | no vendor master or PO chain in the generator; XES→relational conversion |
| Capacity | **no** | Cincinnati Fleet Services (public domain) | no asset/downtime family in the generator; fleet vocabulary needs remapping |
| Throughput | **no** | AdventureWorks WorkOrder/Routing · BPI 2018 (CC0) | no work-order family; cost variance must be synthesized; person-grain quarantine unbuilt |
| Capital | **partial** (payables side) | AdventureWorks inventory + costed transactions | no inventory, no AR; no open AP subledger → DPO needs another source |

The honest read: **five of six dimensions cannot currently be graded at all, and the sixth
is half-grounded.** Public data changes what we can *show*, not what we can *prove*. RFC 3
sequences on that distinction — and closes the "cannot be graded" column with generator
work, because that column is the one thing entirely within our control.

## The generator is a design surface, not a given

The generator emits a ledger today because a ledger is what the first detectors needed. It
is our code, it costs no tokens, no partner and no licence, and it is the only lever that
turns a dimension from demoable into gradeable. Three things follow.

**It has already been extended for exactly this class of work.** `Lever(period_k, factor,
type="price_level")` is a **DGP intervention**, not a post-hoc injection: sale amounts scale
from month *k* and propagate through the whole cascade (AR → receipts → cash/bank → trial
balance → balance sheet). Because the scaling happens after every RNG draw and no control
flow branches on values, a same-seed pair is an **exact counterfactual** — proven by a test
and recorded in `intervention.yaml` next to the data. That is what-if ground truth, built,
and it is the template for every operating lever a dimension needs (price, volume, mix,
rate, timing, capacity).

**The design pattern to keep:** an operating entity is generated from *events* with the
money falling out, not bolted on as a column. That is what makes the existing corpus
internally consistent and traceable, and it is what will make a customer → order → shipment
→ invoice → receipt chain produce a *true* DB1 rather than a plausible one. Truth export
(`ground_truth.yaml`, `metadata_truth.yaml`) is designed with the entities, never after.

**The bias concern is managed, not avoided.** A corpus we invent tests what we thought to
inject — which is why the wild tier exists as the counterweight and why new domains should
borrow *documented real schemas* (ERP/CRM/POS export shapes) rather than schemas we design
from scratch. That is a rule about **where the shape comes from**, not a reason to stay in
one domain. Staying in finance because the generator is currently a ledger would be the
tail wagging the dog: the product is performance analytics, so the generator generates
performance-analytics data.
